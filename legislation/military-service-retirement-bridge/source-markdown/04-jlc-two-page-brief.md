# MILITARY SERVICE RETIREMENT BRIDGE
### Two-page brief for the Joint Leadership Council of Veterans Service Organizations

**Prepared by Policy Development Team using legal, policy, and financial analysis tools, pending review by veterans' legal counsel.** | October 20, 2026 | 2027 General Assembly | Bill version 1.2 | Amends § 2.2-3204 and seven Title 51.1 sections; adds new § 51.1-142.4

## 1. The problem

Virginia already lets public employees buy up to **four years** of prior active duty military service. The right is generous on paper and nearly unusable by those who need it most, because of **when** it is priced. Under § 51.1-142.2 a member buying within the **first 24 months** of employment pays **approximate normal cost** — 12.50% of pay per year in Plan 1, 10.74% in Plan 2; after that the same service costs **actuarial equivalent cost**, several times higher. So the cheap price goes to the new hire in their twenties, who has neither the money nor a reason to care, and the expensive price to the 52-year-old three years short of an unreduced retirement, who knows exactly what those years are worth. **There is no second door.**

## 2. The proposal in one sentence

Create a **narrow second purchase right** — after the 24-month window closes, at the same approximate normal cost — letting a vested Plan 1 or Plan 2 member use prior active duty military service **only to qualify for an unreduced retirement**, with no increase in the pension amount and no effect on any other VRS benefit.

**Plan 1** (unreduced at **age 50 and 30 years**): bridge years count toward the 30-year test only. *Age 52 with 27 years — three bridge years satisfy it, so the member retires unreduced now rather than at 55, on a benefit still calculated on **27** years.* **Plan 2** (unreduced at the **Rule of 90**): bridge years count **one-for-one** as points, partial years proportionally. *Age 58 with 28 years = 86 points; four bridge years reach 90, two years early, benefit still on **28** years.*

**Guardrails in the bill.** Must be vested (5 years); no purchase once you already qualify; **must still be in service when you retire.** Capped at the shortfall **and** at the acceleration actually achievable, so no one buys credit that moves their date by nothing. Counts inside the existing four-year military cap, never increases your age on paper, and is not creditable service for any other purpose. Full-value purchase at actuarial cost stays open and convertible later. A written VRS estimate is required before signing, with money back plus interest if the credit proves unusable.

## 3. What stays untouched, and how the bill guarantees it

The pension amount is protected by one move: the bill **expressly excludes bridge credit from the statutory definition of "creditable service"** in § 51.1-124.3. Because every VRS benefit formula and service threshold runs on creditable service, that single amendment automatically protects the benefit formula, average final compensation, the health insurance credit's threshold and amount, group life thresholds, disability allowances and vesting.

The definition alone was **not enough.** Several provisions key off the **date** of first unreduced eligibility, which the bridge moves, so each was closed by hand: **PLOP** (§ 51.1-165.01), by freezing that date; **death-in-service** (§ 51.1-162), whose survivor allowance turns on it; **deferred retirement** (§ 51.1-153 C), added in version 1.2; and **Workforce Transition Act severance** (**§ 2.2-3204, Title 2.2**), where severance years convert from *age* into *creditable service* — and so into the pension itself — once an employee is "eligible for unreduced retirement." Because the bill's catch-alls stop at the Title 51.1 line, that one needed closing separately. **COLA** (§ 51.1-166) is amended too, but that door was essentially never open and the real exposure is **not** closed. **Accumulated contributions** is also amended, so a member's own payment is refundable.

## 4. The decisions already resolved

**Resolved October 19, 2026**, and implemented in the draft: hybrid plan members are **not** covered — Plan 1 and Plan 2 only (B 1, J); career military retirees are **not** excepted from the retired-pay bar, which the bill inherits (H 5), so the realistic beneficiaries are veterans who separated **without** retirement-eligible service, plus reservists and Guard members protected by 10 U.S.C. § 12736 — **the most important expectation to manage with members**; the **PLOP eligibility date is frozen** (§ 51.1-165.01; F 6); and a **partial purchase capped at the shortfall** is allowed (C 2).

**The PLOP trade-off, disclosed as directed.** PLOP eligibility turns on creditable service *after* first unreduced eligibility, and the bill freezes that date as though the bridge did not exist. **A member who buys bridge years and retires at the accelerated date will generally have no PLOP at all**; one who works through to normal retirement date keeps full PLOP under B 1(i). That is the deliberate price of leaving PLOP untouched; the bill makes VRS disclose it in writing before purchase.

### The three blockers from red-team adversarial review — resolved October 20, 2026

**1. Buy, resign, defer, collect unreduced at 50 — closed.** § 51.1-153 C lets a member who terminates with five or more years retire under subsection B with no in-service requirement, and a deferred member's service is frozen. Buying four years at 44 with 26 years, then resigning, would have bought an unreduced allowance at 50 reachable otherwise only at **65** — roughly **15 years** of acceleration for $35,000, the bill's largest unpriced exposure. **Decision: credit counts only if the member is still in service at retirement** (new E 3, plus an amendment to § 51.1-153 C itself, necessary because that subsection otherwise waives in-service requirements outright). A member who leaves early gets her money back with interest, disclosed in writing before purchase.

**2. The health insurance credit and COLA both start up to four years earlier — accepted, priced, disclosed.** Both key off the **date of retirement**, so no exclusion clause reaches them: the bill bars bridge credit from establishing or increasing the health insurance credit, but neither verb reaches **duration** — about **$5,300** per state retiree with 26 years — and ordinary COLA runs from the earlier retirement and compounds for life. **An earlier version of this brief listed both as protected. That was wrong.** **Decision: accept and disclose rather than cure**, since delaying them would give veterans worse ancillary treatment than other early retirees. The bill says so on its face and makes the Board report both by trust. **Raise these in committee first** — COLA is the one figure our model does not quantify.

**3. Local firefighters, police and EMS in "resolution" localities — narrowed.** Where a locality has adopted an irrevocable resolution under § 51.1-153 B 4, § 51.1-169 A makes its public-safety employees **hybrid-ineligible**, so no exclusion in the bill caught them — including 2026 hires — through its most generous pathway. **Both briefs previously said hazardous-duty members were excluded; that was incorrect.** **Decision: exclude only those first hired into VRS on or after January 1, 2014** (new B 1(iv)). That removes the *permanent and growing* population while keeping the bridge for pre-2014 public-safety veterans, so **the covered group is now closed and shrinking across the whole bill** — the most useful single fact for the money committees.

### What it costs

Approximate normal cost prices a year of *accrual*; the bridge sells removal of the actuarial reduction. The cost model (MOO-259) produces two findings that must always travel together.

**Per member, the price does not cover the value** — it recovers **11%–78%**. The maximum-acceleration Plan 1 case pays **$42,500** for **$383,565**, a **$341,065 subsidy at 11.1% recovery**; an actuarially equivalent price would be about **112.8%** of salary per year, not 12.50%. The Plan 1 50/10 reduction is roughly **60%**, so the bridge turns a 40-cent benefit into a dollar benefit for life.

**In aggregate it is very small** — **0.22 bp** state, **0.20 bp** teacher, **0.14 bp** political subdivisions, **0.19 bp combined** over FY2029–FY2038, from about **222 purchasers** against **$26.05 billion** of covered payroll at a mean deficit of **2.2 years**, not four. **To the nearest basis point, no plan's rate changes.**

**Say both or neither.** "No cost" will be contradicted by the Board's § 30-19.1:7 statement; "$341,000 per veteran" without the denominator kills the bill. The frame is **a deliberate, quantified subsidy for a small and closing population, costing employers less than one basis point.** Two points help: the subsidy reaches only members leaving anyway, since one who would have worked on **overpays** in every profile modeled, and the covered group is closed, so the benefit **extinguishes itself**. One does not: no sensitivity makes this a saving, so cost neutrality is unavailable. Expect the **§ 2.2-3204** comparison — the Commonwealth already buys eligibility, at 15% of compensation.

**What a member gives up by retiring earlier** — not drafting errors but consequences of leaving early, which members must hear before they buy: generally **no PLOP** at the accelerated date (§ 51.1-165.01 B); death-in-service coverage ends at retirement, so survivors fall under § 51.1-163 rather than the more generous § 51.1-162 B allowance; retiring with 26–29 actual years **loses the $8,000 group life floor**, which requires 30, and starts the retiree 25%-a-year reduction earlier on a lower salary (§ 51.1-505 B); and fewer years of accrual on a lower AFC (§ 51.1-155 A 1).

## 5. Status and next steps

Bill text is at **version 1.2. Every policy question is settled** — the four decisions of October 19 and the three blockers of October 20 — and all are drafted. Legal analysis, source appendix, red-team adversarial review, precedent scan and cost model are **complete**. What is outstanding is no longer JLCVSO's to decide: the **VRS Board statement under § 30-19.1:7**, not yet requested and not substituted for by our model, and the federal tax questions on IRC § 415(n) and 10 U.S.C. § 12736. One caveat: the red-team adversarial review was run by the drafting team, so an independent re-read is advisable.

**Recommended sequence:** re-run the cost model against version 1.2 — both October 20 decisions move it **downward** → tax questions to counsel → patron → DLS → VRS pre-filing briefing, so the § 30-19.1:7 statement is informed rather than adversarial. Two verifications close first: the enactment clauses of 2026 c. 563, and an LIS search confirming no comparable bill exists.
