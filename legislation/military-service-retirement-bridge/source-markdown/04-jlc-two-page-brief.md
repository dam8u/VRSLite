# MILITARY SERVICE RETIREMENT BRIDGE
### Two-page brief for the Joint Leadership Council of Veterans Service Organizations

**Prepared by Policy Development Team using legal, policy, and financial analysis tools, pending review by veterans' legal counsel.** | October 20, 2026 | 2027 General Assembly | Package version 1.3 — External-Review Cleanup; operative bill text unchanged at version 1.2 | Amends § 2.2-3204 and seven Title 51.1 sections; adds new § 51.1-142.4

**1. The problem.** Virginia already lets public employees buy up to **four years** of prior active duty military service, but the right is nearly unusable by those who need it most because of **when** it is priced. Under § 51.1-142.2 a member buying within the **first 24 months** of employment pays **approximate normal cost** — for ordinary non-hazardous members, 12.50% of pay per year in Plan 1 and 10.74% in Plan 2; after that the same service costs **actuarial equivalent cost**, several times higher. The low-cost window therefore falls where disposable income is typically lowest and retirement eligibility most remote, while the member who can identify the need — the 52-year-old three years short of an unreduced retirement — faces the higher price. **There is no second door.**

**2. The proposal in one sentence.** Create a **narrow second purchase right** — after the 24-month window closes, at the same approximate normal cost — letting a vested Plan 1 or Plan 2 member use prior active duty military service **only to qualify for an unreduced retirement**. It does not increase benefit-calculation service, the pension formula, or any service-credit-based benefit; the ordinary consequences of an earlier retirement date remain applicable.

**Plan 1** (unreduced at **age 50 and 30 years**): bridge years count toward the 30-year test only. *Age 52 with 27 years — three bridge years satisfy it, so the member retires unreduced now rather than at 55, on a benefit still calculated on **27** years.* **Plan 2** (**Rule of 90**): bridge years count **one-for-one** as points, partial years proportionally. *Age 58 with 28 years = 86 points; four bridge years reach 90, two years early, benefit still on **28** years.*

**Guardrails.** Must be vested (5 years); no purchase once you already qualify; **must still be in service when you retire.** Capped at the shortfall **and** at the acceleration actually achievable. Counts inside the existing four-year military cap, never increases your age on paper, and is not creditable service for any other purpose. Full-value purchase at actuarial cost stays open and convertible. A written VRS estimate is required before signing, with money back plus interest if the credit proves unusable.

**3. What stays untouched, and how the bill guarantees it.** The pension amount is protected by one move: the bill **expressly excludes bridge credit from the statutory definition of "creditable service"** in § 51.1-124.3. Because every VRS formula and service threshold runs on creditable service, that one amendment protects the benefit formula, average final compensation, the health insurance credit's threshold and amount, group life thresholds, disability allowances and vesting.

The definition alone was **not enough.** Several provisions key off the **date** of first unreduced eligibility, which the bridge moves, so each was closed by hand: **PLOP** (§ 51.1-165.01), by freezing that date; **death-in-service** (§ 51.1-162), whose survivor allowance turns on it; **deferred retirement** (§ 51.1-153 C), added in version 1.2; and **Workforce Transition Act severance** (**§ 2.2-3204, Title 2.2**), where severance years convert from *age* into *creditable service* — and so into the pension — once an employee is "eligible for unreduced retirement"; the bill's catch-alls stop at the Title 51.1 line, so that needed closing separately. **COLA** (§ 51.1-166) is amended too, but that door was essentially never open and the real exposure is **not** closed. **Accumulated contributions** is amended, so a member's own payment is refundable.

**4. The decisions already resolved.**

**Resolved October 19, 2026**, and implemented: hybrid plan members are **not** covered — Plan 1 and Plan 2 only (B 1, J); career military retirees are **not** excepted from the retired-pay bar, which the bill inherits (H 5), so realistic beneficiaries separated **without** retirement-eligible service — **the key expectation to manage with members**. Federal law protects qualifying service associated with **non-regular Reserve-component retired pay** under **10 U.S.C. chapter 1223**; whether **§ 12736** applies here is **subject to counsel confirmation**, so assert no broader Reserve or Guard protection. Also settled: the **PLOP eligibility date is frozen** (§ 51.1-165.01; F 6), and a **partial purchase capped at the shortfall** is allowed (C 2).

**The PLOP trade-off, disclosed as directed.** PLOP eligibility turns on creditable service *after* first unreduced eligibility, and the bill freezes that date as though the bridge did not exist. **A member who retires at the accelerated date will generally have no PLOP at all**; one who works through to normal retirement date keeps it in full. That is the deliberate price of leaving PLOP untouched; VRS must disclose it in writing before purchase.

**The three blockers from internal adversarial review — resolved October 20, 2026.**

**1. Buy, resign, defer, collect unreduced at 50 — closed.** § 51.1-153 C lets a member who terminates with five or more years retire under subsection B with no in-service requirement, and a deferred member's service is frozen. Buying four years at 44 with 26 years, then resigning, would have bought an unreduced allowance at 50 otherwise reachable only at **65** — roughly **15 years** of acceleration for $35,000, the bill's largest unpriced exposure. **Decision: credit counts only if the member is still in service at retirement** (new E 3, plus an amendment to § 51.1-153 C itself, since that subsection otherwise waives in-service requirements outright). A member who leaves early is refunded with interest.

**2. The health insurance credit and COLA both start up to four years earlier — accepted, priced, disclosed.** **HIC and COLA follow the retirement date, so preventing earlier commencement would require separate statutory changes outside the bridge** — to Chapter 14 and to § 51.1-166. The bill bars bridge credit from establishing or increasing the health insurance credit, but neither verb reaches **duration** (≈ **$5,300** per state retiree with 26 years), and ordinary COLA runs from the earlier retirement and compounds for life. **An earlier version of this brief listed both as protected. That was wrong.** **The proposal deliberately accepts and discloses the earlier commencement rather than adding separate delay provisions**, since delay would treat veterans worse than other early retirees. The bill says so on its face and makes the Board report both by trust. **Raise these in committee first** — COLA is the one figure our model does not quantify.

**3. Local firefighters, police and EMS in "resolution" localities — narrowed.** Where a locality adopted an irrevocable resolution under § 51.1-153 B 4, § 51.1-169 A makes its public-safety employees **hybrid-ineligible**, so no exclusion caught them — including 2026 hires — through the bill's most generous pathway. **Both briefs previously said hazardous-duty members were excluded; that was incorrect.** **Decision: exclude qualifying B 4 employees who first entered the VRS-administered retirement system on or after the January 1, 2014 Hybrid transition** (new B 1(iv), tracking the § 51.1-169 A membership test, so a member already in a VRS-administered plan before that date stays potentially eligible even if rehired later). That removes the *permanent and growing* population while keeping the bridge for pre-2014 public-safety veterans, so **the covered group is closed and shrinking**.

**What it costs.** Approximate normal cost prices a year of *accrual*; the bridge sells removal of the actuarial reduction. The cost model (MOO-259) produces two findings that must travel together.

**Per member, the price does not cover the value** — it recovers **11%–78%**. In the maximum-acceleration Plan 1 profile the member pays **$42,500** for **$383,565**: **$341,065** is the **maximum modeled net value above the member purchase price under the frozen-service/subsidy scenario** — the single most favorable modeled profile, **not** a cost per veteran. A member who would otherwise have worked on to unreduced eligibility **overpays** in every modeled work-to-eligibility scenario. An actuarially equivalent price would be ≈ **112.8%** of salary per year, against the 12.50% ordinary non-hazardous Plan 1 rate.

**In aggregate it is very small** — **0.22 bp** state, **0.20 bp** teacher, **0.14 bp** political subdivisions, **0.19 bp combined** over FY2029–FY2038, from about **222 purchasers** against **$26.05 billion** of covered payroll at a mean deficit of **2.2 years**, not four. **To the nearest basis point, no plan's rate changes.** That figure is the **modeled retirement-plan employer contribution impact and excludes the separately funded health insurance credit and COLA effects, including unquantified COLA exposure.**

**Say both or neither.** "No cost" will be contradicted by the Board's § 30-19.1:7 statement; a per-member subsidy figure without the denominator kills the bill. The frame is **a deliberate, quantified subsidy for a small and closing population, costing employers less than one basis point.** The subsidy reaches only members leaving anyway, and the covered group is closed, so the benefit **extinguishes itself**. No sensitivity makes this a saving, so cost neutrality is unavailable. Expect the **§ 2.2-3204** comparison — the Commonwealth already buys eligibility, at 15% of compensation.

**What a member gives up by retiring earlier** — ordinary consequences of an earlier retirement date, not drafting errors, which members must hear before they buy: generally **no PLOP** (§ 51.1-165.01 B); death-in-service coverage ends at retirement, so survivors fall under § 51.1-163 rather than § 51.1-162 B; retiring with 26–29 actual years **loses the $8,000 group life floor**, which requires 30, and starts the retiree 25%-a-year reduction earlier on a lower salary (§ 51.1-505 B); and fewer years of accrual on a lower AFC (§ 51.1-155 A 1).

**5. Status and next steps.** Operative bill text is at **version 1.2**; the package is at **version 1.3 — External-Review Cleanup**. **All principal policy questions are settled** — the four decisions of October 19 and the three blockers of October 20 — and all are drafted. Legal analysis, source appendix, internal adversarial review and precedent scan are **complete**; the cost model is **complete for version 1.1, and a version 1.2 refresh is required before external use**. **One narrow question is unresolved:** how the in-service-at-retirement requirement applies to approved leave or long-term disability — resolve or expressly defer it before patron use. Also outstanding, and not JLCVSO's to decide: the **VRS Board statement under § 30-19.1:7**, not requested and not substituted for by our model, and the federal tax questions on IRC § 415(n) and 10 U.S.C. § 12736. The internal adversarial review was run by the policy-development team, so an independent re-read is advisable.

**Recommended sequence:** re-run the cost model against version 1.2 — both October 20 decisions move it **downward** → tax questions to counsel → patron → DLS → VRS pre-filing briefing. Two verifications close first: the enactment clauses of 2026 c. 563, and an LIS search confirming no comparable bill exists.
