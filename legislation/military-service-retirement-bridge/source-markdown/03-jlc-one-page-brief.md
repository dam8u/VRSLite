# MILITARY SERVICE RETIREMENT BRIDGE
### One-page brief for the Joint Leadership Council of Veterans Service Organizations

**Proposal:** Let a vested VRS Plan 1 or Plan 2 member use up to four years of prior active duty military service, at the "approximate normal cost" new hires pay, **but only to reach the finish line for an unreduced retirement** — never to raise the pension check. An **eligibility benefit, not pension accrual.**

**The gap this fills.** A veteran has **24 months** from hire to buy up to four years of active duty service at approximate normal cost — for ordinary non-hazardous members, 12.50% of pay a year in Plan 1, 10.74% in Plan 2. After that the same years cost **actuarial equivalent cost**, several times more. That window falls early in a career, when income is often lower and retirement remote; the 52-year-old three years short is priced out. **A pricing cliff, no second door.**

**What the proposal does.** Existing right untouched. The bridge is available **any time after the 24-month window, once vested**, at the **same price**, and buys **eligibility only**. **Plan 1** — counts toward the 30 years for unreduced retirement at 50 or later: *age 52 + 27 years + 3 bridge years = 30, unreduced now instead of at 55, benefit still on 27 years.* **Plan 2** — one-for-one toward the Rule of 90: *age 58 + 28 + 4 bridge years = 90, two years early, benefit still on 28 years.* Capped at the shortfall and achievable acceleration; inside the **four-year military cap**.

**What it does not change, and what it does.** It does **not** increase benefit-calculation service, the pension formula, or any service-credit-based benefit, secured by express exclusions plus six conforming amendments. **But ordinary consequences of an earlier retirement date remain applicable — say this first.** The **health insurance credit** and **COLA** follow the retirement date, so preventing earlier commencement would need separate statutory changes outside the bridge; both begin up to four years earlier, group life steps down earlier, and retiring ends death-in-service coverage. Resolved October 20: **accept and disclose.**

### Decisions resolved October 19–20, 2026; independent review integrated

Hybrid members **not** covered (Plan 1/Plan 2 only); **PLOP date frozen**; **partial purchase capped at the shortfall**; credit counts **only if the member retires directly from service**, closing a buy-resign-defer path worth ~15 years; HIC and COLA costs **accepted and disclosed**; § 51.1-153 B 4 public-safety employees entering VRS **on or after January 1, 2014** are **excluded**. Call the group **substantially closing**, not "closed and shrinking." **Open:** whether approved leave counts as remaining in service. **Career military retirees are generally shut out — manage this:** the bill inherits the bar on buying service counted in another retirement allowance, including military retired pay. **10 U.S.C. § 12736 does not preempt this bill.**

**The PLOP trade-off.** PLOP runs on service performed *after* first unreduced eligibility, and the bill freezes that date as if the bridge did not exist, so **a member retiring at the accelerated date will generally have no PLOP;** one who works on keeps it in full.

**Carry this into the money committees — it costs money, and lead with that.** The price recovers **11%–78%** of the acceleration's value. **$341,065** is the **modeled net value above the purchase price in the single most favorable published profile** — not a cost per veteran, and not a maximum (the cell grid reaches ~$419,000). Employer rate impact is **0.19 bp combined** over FY2029–FY2038, from ~**222 purchasers** against **$26.05bn** of payroll, so **to the nearest basis point no plan's rate changes.** That is the **modeled retirement-plan employer contribution impact and excludes the separately funded health insurance credit and COLA effects, including unquantified COLA exposure.** Carry both halves: "no cost" will be contradicted by the Board's § 30-19.1:7 statement.
