# MILITARY SERVICE RETIREMENT BRIDGE
### One-page brief for the Joint Leadership Council of Veterans Service Organizations

**Proposal:** Let a vested VRS Plan 1 or Plan 2 member use up to four years of prior active duty military service, at the same low "approximate normal cost" new hires pay, **but only to reach the finish line for an unreduced retirement** — never to raise the pension check. An **eligibility benefit, not a pension-accrual benefit**: it lets a veteran who has already given Virginia a full career finish sooner.

### The gap this fills

A veteran hired into Virginia service has **24 months** to buy up to four years of active duty military service at approximate normal cost (12.50% of pay per year in Plan 1, 10.74% in Plan 2). After that the same four years cost **actuarial equivalent cost** — routinely tens of thousands of dollars. The cheap price goes to people who do not yet know they need it; the 52-year-old three years short of an unreduced retirement is priced out. **A pricing cliff with no second door.**

### What the proposal does

The existing right is untouched: first 24 months after hire, approximate normal cost, full service credit that **does** raise your pension. The new bridge is available **any time after, once vested**, at the **same price**, and buys **eligibility only** — it adds **nothing** to the benefit formula. **Plan 1:** counts toward the 30 years needed for unreduced retirement at 50 or later — *age 52 + 27 years + 3 bridge years = 30, unreduced now instead of at 55, benefit still on 27 years.* **Plan 2:** one-for-one toward the Rule of 90 — *age 58 + 28 years + 4 bridge years = 90, two years early, benefit still on 28 years.* Capped at the shortfall and at the acceleration achievable; inside the existing **four-year lifetime military cap**; never makes you older on paper. Full-value purchase at actuarial cost stays available.

**Protected** by express exclusions plus six conforming amendments: vesting · benefit formula · AFC · PLOP · disability · death-in-service · group life · portability.

**Not protected — say so first.** Retiring up to four years earlier starts the **health insurance credit** and the **COLA** up to four years earlier. Both key off the retirement date, not service credit, so no exclusion clause reaches them — a real recurring cost on two separately funded trusts. The Council chose to **accept and disclose** these rather than delay them, which would give veterans worse ancillary treatment than other early retirees.

### Decisions JLCVSO made, October 19–20, 2026 — all seven are settled

Hybrid members **not** covered (Plan 1 / Plan 2 only); no exception to the retired-pay bar; **PLOP date frozen**, trade-off disclosed; **partial purchase capped at the shortfall**; credit counts **only if the member is still working at retirement**, closing a buy-resign-defer loophole worth ~15 years of acceleration; health insurance credit and COLA costs **accepted and disclosed**; and B 4 "resolution" public-safety employees **first hired on or after January 1, 2014 excluded** — so the covered group is **closed and shrinking**.

**The PLOP trade-off, stated plainly.** PLOP eligibility depends on service performed *after* the date you first became eligible for an unreduced retirement, and the bill freezes that date as if the bridge did not exist. **A member who buys bridge years and retires at the accelerated date will generally have no PLOP** — no lump sum. One who works on to normal retirement age keeps PLOP in full. That is the deliberate price of leaving PLOP untouched, and the bill requires VRS to say so in writing before a member signs.

### What to carry into the money committees

**It costs money — lead with that.** The cost model (MOO-259) puts both halves on the record: the price recovers **11%–78%** of what the acceleration is worth, reaching a **$341,065 subsidy** for the maximum-acceleration Plan 1 member, while the employer rate impact is **0.19 basis points combined** over FY2029–FY2038 — ~**222 purchasers** against **$26.05 billion** of payroll, so **to the nearest basis point no plan's rate changes.** Carry both together: "no cost" will be contradicted by the Board's § 30-19.1:7 statement, and "$341,000 per veteran" without the denominator kills the bill. Frame it as a **deliberate, quantified subsidy for a small and closing population, costing employers under one basis point.**

**Manage this expectation with members: career military retirees are generally shut out.** The bill inherits the bar on buying service already counted in another retirement allowance, which includes military retired pay; Reserve and Guard service is protected by federal law. The realistic beneficiary is the veteran who separated **without** retirement-eligible service — not the 20-year retiree, whom members will assume is covered.

**Status: settled on policy; two items close before a patron.** Obtain the Board's mandatory **§ 30-19.1:7** statement — our cost model is illustrative, **not** a substitute — and put the federal tax questions to counsel.
