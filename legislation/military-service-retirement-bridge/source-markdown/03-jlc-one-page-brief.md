# MILITARY SERVICE RETIREMENT BRIDGE
### One-page brief for the Joint Leadership Council of Veterans Service Organizations

**Proposal:** Let a vested VRS Plan 1 or Plan 2 member use up to four years of prior active duty military service, at the "approximate normal cost" new hires pay, **but only to reach the finish line for an unreduced retirement** — never to raise the pension check. An **eligibility benefit, not a pension-accrual benefit**.

**The gap this fills.** A veteran has **24 months** from hire to buy up to four years of active duty military service at approximate normal cost — for ordinary non-hazardous members, 12.50% of pay per year in Plan 1, 10.74% in Plan 2. After that the same years cost **actuarial equivalent cost**, several times higher. The low-cost window falls early in a career, when disposable income is often lower and retirement eligibility is remote; the 52-year-old three years short is priced out. **A pricing cliff, no second door.**

**What the proposal does.** Existing right untouched. The bridge is available **any time after the 24-month window, once vested**, at the **same price**, and buys **eligibility only**. **Plan 1** — counts toward the 30 years for unreduced retirement at 50 or later: *age 52 + 27 years + 3 bridge years = 30, unreduced now instead of at 55, benefit still on 27 years.* **Plan 2** — one-for-one toward the Rule of 90: *age 58 + 28 years + 4 bridge years = 90, two years early, benefit still on 28 years.* Capped at the shortfall and at the acceleration achievable; inside the existing **four-year military cap**.

**What it does and does not change.** It does **not** increase benefit-calculation service, the pension formula, or any service-credit-based benefit — secured by express exclusions plus six conforming amendments. **Ordinary consequences of an earlier retirement date remain applicable.**

**Say this first.** The **health insurance credit** and the **COLA** follow the retirement date, so preventing earlier commencement would require separate statutory changes outside the bridge. Both begin up to four years earlier. Resolved October 20, 2026: the proposal **deliberately accepts and discloses** that commencement rather than adding separate delay provisions.

### Decisions resolved October 19–20, 2026 — all principal policy questions settled

Hybrid members **not** covered (Plan 1/Plan 2 only); no retired-pay-bar exception; **PLOP date frozen**; **partial purchase capped at the shortfall**; credit counts **only if the member is still in service at retirement**, closing a buy-resign-defer loophole worth ~15 years; health insurance credit and COLA costs **accepted and disclosed**; and § 51.1-153 B 4 "resolution" public-safety employees who **first entered the VRS-administered retirement system on or after the January 1, 2014 Hybrid transition** are **excluded** — so the group is **closed and shrinking**. **One narrow question stays open:** how the in-service-at-retirement requirement applies to approved leave or long-term disability.

**The PLOP trade-off.** PLOP eligibility depends on service performed *after* first unreduced eligibility, and the bill freezes that date as if the bridge did not exist. **A member who retires at the accelerated date will generally have no PLOP;** one who works on keeps it in full. VRS must disclose this in writing before purchase.

**Carry this into the money committees — it costs money, and lead with that.** The price recovers **11%–78%** of the acceleration's value. **$341,065** is the **maximum modeled net value above the member purchase price under the frozen-service/subsidy scenario** — the most favorable single profile, **not** a cost per veteran; members who would otherwise have worked on to unreduced eligibility **overpay** in every modeled work-to-eligibility case. Employer rate impact is **0.19 basis points combined** over FY2029–FY2038 — ~**222 purchasers** against **$26.05 billion** of payroll, so **to the nearest basis point no plan's rate changes.** That is the **modeled retirement-plan employer contribution impact and excludes the separately funded health insurance credit and COLA effects, including unquantified COLA exposure.** Carry both halves: "no cost" will be contradicted by the Board's § 30-19.1:7 statement.

**Career military retirees are generally shut out — manage this expectation.** The bill inherits the bar on buying service counted in another retirement allowance, which includes military retired pay. Federal law protects qualifying service associated with **non-regular Reserve-component retired pay** under **10 U.S.C. chapter 1223**; whether **§ 12736** applies to this bridge is **subject to counsel confirmation**.
