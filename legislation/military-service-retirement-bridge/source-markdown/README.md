## Core policy principle

The bridge is an **eligibility benefit, not a pension-accrual benefit**. It lets a vested VRS Plan 1 or Plan 2 member use up to four years of prior active duty military service, at approximate normal cost after the existing 24-month window has closed, **solely** to reach the threshold for an unreduced retirement allowance — with no increase in the retirement allowance and no effect on any other VRS benefit.

## The three moves that make it work

1. **New § 51.1-142.4**, not an amendment inside § 51.1-142.2 — because "creditable service" is defined as "prior service as set forth in § 51.1-142.2 plus membership service," so anything added there becomes creditable service automatically.
2. **Express carve-out from the "creditable service" definition** in § 51.1-124.3, which protects the benefit formula, AFC, the health insurance credit's **threshold and amount** (not its commencement date — see below), group life thresholds, disability allowances, and the five-year vesting analogue in one stroke.
3. **Six conforming amendments across five sections** — to §§ 51.1-162, 51.1-165.01 and 51.1-166, which key off the *date of unreduced-retirement eligibility* rather than off creditable service; to **§ 2.2-3204 in Title 2.2** (version 1.1), under which Workforce Transition Act severance years convert from *age* into *creditable service* once an employee is eligible for unreduced retirement; and to **§ 51.1-153 C** (version 1.2), the deferred-retirement subsection, which expressly waives in-service requirements and would otherwise have swallowed the new in-service-at-retirement condition. Without these, the credit leaks into PLOP, the death-in-service benefit, deferred retirement, and an employer-funded increase in the allowance itself, even though it is not creditable service.

**What the three moves do not reach.** They control what the credit *is*. They do not control what an **earlier retirement date** does. The health insurance credit and ordinary COLA both commence at retirement, so both start up to four years earlier and neither is reachable by exclusion drafting. **Per DECISION 9 that cost is accepted, priced and disclosed rather than cured**, and the savings paragraph at the end of § 51.1-142.4 F says so affirmatively so no one misreads subdivision F 8 as delaying commencement.

## Policy decisions — all seven settled

See `02-legal-analysis-memo.docx`, Part 6.

**Settled October 19, 2026:** hybrid plan participants excluded (Plan 1 / Plan 2 only); the military retired pay bar inherited without exception; the PLOP eligibility date frozen, with the trade-off disclosed in both briefs; partial purchase capped at the shortfall permitted.

**Settled October 20, 2026 — the three blockers from independent review:**

- **DECISION 8 — deferred-member acceleration.** *Require member-in-service status at retirement, not only at purchase.* Implemented in new § 51.1-142.4 E 3 **and** in an amendment to § 51.1-153 C — both were necessary, because that subsection's general waiver of in-service requirements would otherwise have consumed the new condition. A member who leaves before retiring is refunded with interest (K 1 c, K 1 d) and must be told so in writing before purchase (I 2(vii)).
- **DECISION 9 — health insurance credit and COLA commencement.** *Accept and price both; disclose them in the briefs and to the money committees.* No exclusion is attempted because none is possible. Clause 3(vi) of the enactment requires the Board to report both by trust. The COLA exposure is the one figure the cost model does not quantify.
- **DECISION 10 — § 51.1-153 B 4 public-safety scope.** *Exclude only those whose first VRS membership began on or after January 1, 2014.* Implemented in new clause B 1(iv) and subsection J, with new subdivision C 1 c resolving which deficit formula applies to the still-eligible pre-2014 class. Narrower than counsel recommended and adopted without reservation: the defect was that the class was permanent and growing, and this removes exactly that while keeping the bridge for pre-2014 public-safety veterans. **The beneficiary class is now closed and shrinking across the entire bill.** The Council's wording was conformed to the statute without changing its substance; its verbatim text is recorded in Part 6.1 of the memorandum.

**Remaining open items are not JLCVSO policy questions:** the VRS Board's § 30-19.1:7 statement, the federal tax questions in Part 5.2 of the memorandum, a re-run of the cost model against version 1.2, and the two verification items in Part 7.5. Drafting note 10 also flags one sub-question the Council has not been asked — whether a member on approved leave or long-term disability at retirement should count as in service for DECISION 8 purposes.

## What it costs

ACTUARY 1 and ACTUARY 2 are **answered** (MOO-259). Two numbers, and the package must always carry both:

- **Per member, the price does not cover the value.** Approximate normal cost recovers **11% to 78%** of the value of the acceleration. The maximum-acceleration Plan 1 case pays **$42,500** for **$383,565** — a **$341,065 subsidy at 11.1% recovery**. The bill is **not cost neutral and must not be presented as cost neutral.**
- **In aggregate it is very small.** Employer contribution rate impact over FY2029–FY2038: **0.22 bp** state, **0.20 bp** teacher, **0.14 bp** political subdivisions, **0.19 bp** combined — about **222 purchasers** against **$26.05 billion** of covered payroll. To the nearest basis point, no plan's rate changes. Across every sensitivity tested the aggregate stays between **0.05 and 0.44 bp**.

Three things that follow. The § 51.1-124.3 carve-out is worth a **5.7×** difference in the liability increase and is the single most important provision in the bill. A member who would have worked on to unreduced eligibility **overpays** in every profile modeled, so the subsidy exists only for members who were leaving anyway. And hybrid inclusion would change the ten-year number by **less than one hundredth of a basis point**, so DECISION 2 stands on equity and precedent, not on near-term cost.

**Three limits on that model, stated plainly.** It is an illustrative public-finance model, **not** an actuarial certification and **not** the VRS Board's mandatory statement under § 30-19.1:7, which has not been requested. Its weakest input is the veteran-population funnel — one published anchor and four assumed filters — which alone moves the aggregate from 0.07 to 0.44 bp. And it was built against **version 1.1**, so it should be re-run: it does not price the DECISION 8 deferred path, which version 1.2 has now **closed**, and its population includes the post-2014 B 4 public-safety employees whom clause B 1(iv) now **excludes**. Both corrections run downward, so no figure above is an understatement on their account.
