## Verdict

**Twenty-seven findings: three blockers, eight material, eight moderate, eight minor.** Twenty-three of the twenty-seven are framing defects. That ratio is not a comment on care taken; it is what this artifact is. It does not compute anything, so it cannot be arithmetically wrong — it can only be narratively wrong, and it is, in three places that matter.

**Every figure the model produces checks.** This is worth stating first and plainly, because it is the part that was done well and because it bounds the rest of the review. I re-derived the quoted figures from the engine independently of the harness, and all of them close: the central case at **$7,151,771 / 0.1888 bp**; the full φ ladder (**$17.7377M / 0.4682 bp** at φ = 0, **$12.4447M / 0.3285**, **$7.1518M / 0.1888**, **$1.8588M / 0.0491**, **−$3.4341M / −0.0906**) and its zero crossing at **φ = 0.8378**; the take-up ladder (**55.5 / 111.0 / 221.9 / 332.9 / 554.9** participants at 0.0472 / 0.0944 / 0.1888 / 0.2832 / 0.4719 bp); the board's worked question at **$12.8755M / 0.3399 bp / $15.7981M payroll / +$2.9226M combined**, identical in the pension column at ρ = 100%; the compound-adverse case at **$43.7863M / 1.1558 bp**; the funnel head at **366,289** and its six narrowing screens with provenance exactly as described; **$403,803** for the health insurance credit; **−$574,123** for the Plan 2 aggregate; **$929,400 / 0.0245 bp** low and **780.9 participants / $25.1633M / 0.6642 bp** high. Profile A returns **+$341,065** and **−$21,500** to the dollar. I could not find a quoted number that the model does not produce at the stated settings. The one arithmetic defect anywhere in the document is a hand-computed illustration that no panel asserts and no test can reach — finding **m-08**.

**What fails is the distance between a correct number and what the reader is told it means — and the specific failure mode is that the walk-through is systematically thinner than the panels it narrates.** Three times, the dashboard carries a qualifier and the walk-through drops it:

- Panel E prints `$341,065` as *"the most favorable of the seven published profiles—not a cost per veteran and not a model-wide maximum"* and gives the grid's ~$419,000 ceiling. **Step 3 carries none of that**, and labels the figure with the one word — *"frozen-service"* — that package version 1.4 formally withdrew for this figure.
- Panel F prints *"φ guard band: ASM-22's documented range is 0.25–0.75, while the control and this tornado span 0–1."* **Step 8 carries none of that**, and draws a ranking conclusion from bars whose swing bases it never discloses.
- Panel D captions tile 2 *"an employer compensation effect — **not** a pension saving"* and tile 3 *"the least reliable of the three."* **Step 5's reproduction of Panel D drops both captions**, keeping only the three numbers and the bolded `+$2.92M`.

That pattern matters more than any individual instance. The walk-through is the artifact that prints, that travels as a PDF, and that gets screenshotted. Where the panel is more careful than the narration, the careful version is the one that stays behind.

**Three blockers.** The first is the only one I would call serious on its own terms as a matter of substance rather than presentation: the document's statement of the gap the bill fills — in the third paragraph, in the only sentence every reader reads — misstates current law in the direction that helps the bill. The second and third are the two figures the mandate named, both failing in exactly the way the mandate anticipated.

**On the mandate's own first question — does step 5 land?** Mechanically, yes, and I tried hard to break it. *"Stop at that third row"* renders 81 pixels below the `+$2.92M` row and is visible without scrolling in the drawer at every viewport down to 1280×800, and it falls on the same printed page as the row on both A4 and US Letter with 304 px and 936 px of page left below. The prose is good: it names the error, names the two assumptions, and gives the counterfactual ($15.80M → zero, combined → a $12.88M cost). **The explanation lands.** What does not land is the table, because the table is the extractable object and it has been stripped of Panel D's own warnings. That is finding **M-02**, not a blocker.

**On the mandate's second question — can a reader reach a free-looking screen by following the instructions?** On the dashboard, no, and this is a genuine strength. Panel D is structurally incapable of printing a favourable headline: three separately-provenanced tiles, tile 2 captioned *not a pension saving*, tile 3 captioned *least reliable of the three*, and a standing note stating that the payroll figure *"depends entirely on a hiring assumption the Commonwealth controls and which this model cannot observe."* Step 3's instruction to drag φ to 100% does produce a positive combined effect of **+$3.43M** — and step 3 says so, explains it, and the panel flags the setting as outside the documented band. I could not construct a path through the walk-through's own instructions to a favourable screen without the caveat on it. The exposure is in the *printed* artifact, not the tool.

**On the mandate's third question — does the step order argue a case?** The ordering is defensible, and the choice to open at *No Workforce Savings* is the right one. But the adversarial characterisation is available and I would make it: the two most favourable framings sit in steps 1 and 2, inside any reader's attention budget, and the three steps carrying the bill's largest disclosed unknowns — the unquantified COLA exposure (7), the sensitivity compounding (8), and the five pre-patron conditions (10) — sit behind a stated twenty-minute read. Step 1 delivers *"to the nearest basis point, no employer's contribution rate changes"* on page 2, before the reader has been told that the sign of the result is undetermined. That is finding **S-06**, and the fix is one clause.

## 1 · The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

*The brief I would write if I were staff to a money-committee member who had decided to kill this bill, having been handed the walk-through and nothing else. Stated as its best advocate would state it.*

> I want to be fair to this document, because it is the most honest thing the proponents have produced and I am going to use it against them anyway. That is not a paradox. It is what happens when you write down your own weaknesses and then walk a legislator past them at the speed of a guided tour.
>
> Start with the premise. Paragraph three, the paragraph that exists to tell you why we need a bill at all: *"under current law that same veteran can buy military service only in her first 24 months of VRS-covered employment."* That is not what the Code says and the proponents' own legal memorandum says so in a table — *"When may it be purchased? **Any time while a member in service. There is no deadline on the right to purchase.**"* What closes at twenty-four months is a **discount**. So the gap this bill fills is not that a veteran cannot buy her service; it is that after two years she has to pay what it is worth. Strip the error out and the bill's entire justification restates as: we would like veterans to buy an early retirement date at roughly a ninth of its actuarial value, and current law already lets them buy it at full price. That is a defensible policy. It is a completely different policy from the one in paragraph three, and I would read paragraph three aloud, then read the memorandum's table aloud, and then ask the patron which of his own documents he would like me to believe.
>
> Now the number they want you to carry. Three hundred forty-one thousand and sixty-five dollars of net value to one veteran. The document tells you it is "illustrative profile A." It does not tell you — though their own dashboard does, and their own README does in bold — that it is **the highest of seven published profiles**, that the next highest is one hundred one thousand, that the average across their published profiles is ninety-three thousand, and that their underlying grid reaches four hundred nineteen thousand. So here is the arithmetic a staffer does in the margin at 11pm: three hundred forty-one thousand times two hundred twenty-two purchasers is **seventy-five million dollars**. Their own aggregate is seven. They are out by a factor of ten in the direction that destroys them, and the document gave the staffer every component of that error and withheld the one sentence that would have prevented it.
>
> Then the sentence they have built the whole fiscal case on, and which I would spend my entire four minutes on. Step 9, last paragraph: *"Even the top of that range does not move any employer's published contribution rate when rounded to the nearest basis point."* Turn to their own cost model memorandum, section 7: *"**At 1.16 bp a plan's rate does change to the nearest basis point**, so this case falsifies the sentence the package quotes most often."* They wrote that. Their own modeller wrote that, about this exact sentence, and then the walk-through asserts the sentence anyway, two paragraphs after quoting the 1.16 figure that falsifies it — and then repeats it in the summary, in the five things they want you to remember. I do not have to prove anything. I have to put two of their own documents side by side on the overhead.
>
> And then the thing nobody will notice until a VRS actuary notices it in front of you. This act takes effect **July 1, 2029**. The model runs the **FY2029** cohort. Fiscal 2029 ended three weeks before the bill does. Their own fiscal memorandum lists "the FY2030–FY2039 window shift" as work still outstanding. So the headline number is computed over a ten-year window whose first year the bill cannot reach, and the walk-through states that window as a bare fact nineteen times without once saying it is stale.
>
> Last, the part they are proudest of, and they should be: they tell you the answer's sign turns on a parameter nobody can measure, that self-selection runs the wrong way, and that their central case should be read as the optimistic end rather than the middle. Good. Now look at where they put it. Not in the opening. Not in the three things they say you must know *before you read a single number*. Those three things are that counts are ten-year totals, that figures carry provenance badges, and that nothing is labelled "savings" — all true, all helpful, none of them the thing that decides whether this bill costs money or makes money. The sign-deciding assumption is on screen four of twelve, behind a figure they have already given you on screen two with a confident qualifier attached. A document that genuinely wanted me to discount its headline would have put φ above the headline. This one put the headline first and the honesty on page four, and then wrote that it was being honest. I believe them. I am still going to say this in the room.

## 4 · Fiscal assumption challenge

**The load-bearing assumption is not φ.** The walk-through says it is, and for the *sign* of the per-member result that is right. But for the claim the document actually asks a committee to act on — *no employer's contribution rate changes to the nearest basis point* — the load-bearing assumption is the **one-at-a-time sensitivity design**, and the walk-through half-discloses it.

**The test.** I re-ran the model with the drivers compounded rather than swung singly.

| Case | Modeled pension impact | Basis points | Does any plan's rate change to the nearest bp? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central, as step 1 presents it | $7,151,771 | 0.1888 | No |
| Widest single-driver excursion (φ = 0) | $17,737,700 | 0.4682 | No |
| High Participation preset (step 9) | $25,163,300 | 0.6642 | No |
| Take-up × funnel compounded (step 8's own figure) | — | 1.107 | **Yes** |
| Published compound-adverse: high funnel, φ = 0.25, 30% take-up (step 9's own figure) | $43,786,300 | **1.1558** | **Yes** |

**The result.** The conclusion the walk-through calls *"the strongest honest claim available from this analysis"* is true on every single-driver excursion and **false on both compound cases the document itself quotes**. The model's own memorandum says so in terms: *"At 1.16 bp a plan's rate does change to the nearest basis point, so this case falsifies the sentence the package quotes most often."* The walk-through quotes the 1.16 figure in step 8 and again in step 9, and then asserts the falsified sentence in step 9's next paragraph and again in the closing. That is finding **B-02**, and it is a framing defect built on a correct number — which is precisely the failure mode the mandate said it cared most about.

**A second challenge, which the walk-through passes.** I tested whether the pension figure is genuinely insulated from the workforce controls, since the whole credibility of step 6 rests on it. It is: at 296/104 participants the modeled pension impact is **$12,875,500 / 0.3399 bp** at ρ = 85% with 5% not replaced, and **$12,875,500 / 0.3399 bp** at ρ = 100% with 5% not replaced — identical, and the tornado reports the two workforce drivers at a swing of exactly 0.0000 bp. **A third, which it also passes.** Step 5 claims that entering counts *"rescales the modeled age, service and salary mix rather than inventing an average member."* Re-derived: 400 direct participants return $12.8755M against a strictly proportional $12.891M, the 0.1% difference being the 296/104 split against the model's own 74.06% Plan 1 share. ## 7 · Disposition

**Three blockers, eight material, eight moderate, eight minor.**

**The single most serious finding is B-01**, because it is the only one that is wrong about the world rather than wrong about emphasis. The document's statement of the gap the bill fills — in paragraph three, in the one sentence every reader reads, and in the direction that helps the bill — says a veteran *cannot buy* her military service after twenty-four months. She can. What she loses is the price. The proponents' own legal memorandum says so in a table, and a VRS reviewer will reach that table before lunch. The fix is five words and a corrected pinpoint.

**B-02 is the one that would lose a hearing.** The sentence the document calls the strongest honest claim available is a sentence the package's own fiscal model says is falsified by a case the document quotes two paragraphs earlier. It is then repeated in the five things the reader is asked to remember. Nothing about this is hidden — both documents are in the package and both say what they say. That is why it is dangerous: an opponent does not have to find anything, only to put two pages side by side.

**B-03 is the one that produces a wrong number in someone else's mouth.** $341,065 appears once, on page 4 of the printed artifact, with every component of a ten-fold error available and the one preventing sentence removed — a sentence the dashboard already prints.

Thirteen of the twenty-seven findings are a single recurring defect in two forms: a qualifier that lives on a panel and not in the narration, or a figure that travels further than its caveat. The document is unusually honest about its own weaknesses — step 3's φ disclosure and step 7's COLA disclosure are better than anything I expected to find, and I would not change a word of either. The problem is placement and transit, not candour. Everything in section 2 is fixable in prose, inside `js/walkthrough.js`, with no change to the engine and no reopening of any settled policy decision. Two findings (**M-07**, **M-08**) touch `js/ui.js` and belong to Drafting rather than to this document.

**For the memo's risk section, explicitly:** **B-01**, **B-02**, **B-03**, **S-04** and **S-08**. The first three because they are what an opponent uses; **S-04** because the bill discloses three member detriments that the stakeholder-facing document does not; **S-08** because an actuary will find the fiscal-year window and it should be disclosed rather than discovered.

**This document should not go outside until B-01, B-02 and B-03 are fixed.** That is a recommendation, not a hold — whether to proceed is the Policy Director's call, and I hold none of this work.
