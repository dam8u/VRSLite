1 · What this model is, and what it is not

This page states every formula the Explorer uses, in the order the calculation actually runs, with the statutory provision or published source behind it. It is written so that a VRS actuary, a Division of Legislative Services drafter, or money-committee staff can check the arithmetic without reading JavaScript — and so that anyone who disagrees with a number can see exactly which assumption to argue with.

If you have not used the dashboard before, start with the guided walk-through instead — it names the settings to choose, in order, and says what each screen means. It is also available inside the dashboard itself, in a panel beside the controls, so you do not need two windows open. This page is the reference behind it.

The model answers one question: if a stated number of Plan 1 and Plan 2 veterans purchase eligibility credit under the proposed bridge, what is the modeled change in pension liability, in the employer contribution rate, and — separately — in employer payroll? It does not answer whether the bridge is good policy, and it is not built to produce a favourable total.

It is not an actuarial certification, it is not the VRS Board impact statement required by Va. Code § 30-19.1:7 (which is mandatory for this bill and has not been requested), and it is not a General Assembly fiscal impact statement. Where this model and an official VRS statement disagree, the official statement governs and this model is wrong.

Bill version modelled — read this before quoting any figure

The eligibility, purchase-cap and scope mechanics in §§ 5 and 6 below implement the bill version 1.2 operative text: partial purchase capped at the deficit (rule D4a), hybrid participants excluded, and the PLOP and COLA eligibility dates frozen. The aggregate tables this engine is calibrated against were produced for bill version 1.1 — complete for version 1.1; a refresh against the bill as introduced is required before external use. The operative bill text is now at version 1.4.

Three scope changes made after the calibration all move the modeled result downward, so no figure here is an understatement on their account:

  • The in-service-at-retirement condition (subdivision E 3) closes an acceleration path the model never priced. Version 1.3 correction: the version 1.2 drafting did not in fact close that path — it tested a status on one named day, and that status was purchasable. Version 1.3 attaches the condition to continuity of service from the date of grant. On the version 1.2 text the aggregates were understatements on this account; on the version 1.3 text they are not.
  • Clause B 1(iv) removes post-2013 § 51.1-153 B 4 public-safety entrants, a population the model counted.
  • Clause B 1(v), added in version 1.3, removes persons who participate in or are merely eligible to participate in the optional retirement plans under §§ 51.1-126.1, 51.1-126.3, 51.1-126.4 and 51.1-126.7. This model does not apply that screen, so its population is overstated to that extent. The amount is expected to be immaterial but has not been quantified.

That is a direction, not a quantification, and it is not a substitute for the refresh.

The one-sentence warning that matters most

The behavioural assumption φ (§ 9) decides the sign of the per-member result, and no published data bears on it. A reader who takes one number away from this tool should take the range across φ, not the central case.

And the shipped file is tested as a document, not as a module

Reconciling the engine says nothing about the file a legislator is actually sent. A control can be wired to the wrong parameter, display one value and feed the model another, or leave a panel holding a stale number, and a test that imports js/engine.js directly would pass throughout. So node test/standalone.js opens dist/explorer-standalone.html in a real headless browser, drives every slider, numeric entry, checkbox, select, mode segment and preset the way a user does, and for each resulting state:

  1. checks the control’s own displayed value against the value it holds — which is what catches a preset asking for a setting that its slider cannot express;
  2. reconstructs the engine parameters from the visible controls alone, independently of the interface’s internal state, runs the model on them, and checks that every figure on every panel is the one the model returned;
  3. asserts the fiscal guardrails and the dimensional sanity checks — a vacancy of n months must cost n months of pay, the population funnel must never widen, no Panel C control may move any pension figure — which hold regardless of what the engine says.

It runs 2,086 checks in twenty-one groups and currently passes all of them, including an assertion that the inlined payloads are byte-identical to the current sources, so a stale dist/ fails loudly rather than being tested in place of the real application. The one dependency is a locally installed Chrome or Chromium, reached over the DevTools protocol with Node’s built-in WebSocket; no npm package is added.

And the walk-through is checked against the model it narrates

The guided walk-through quotes about forty figures — “$7.15M”, “0.19 bp”, “$341,065” and so on — and prose is exactly the kind of thing that keeps its old numbers after a model changes. So every one of those figures is recorded in js/walkthrough.js alongside the settings that produce it, and group 19 of the harness drives the shipped document to those settings and requires the figure to be rendered on the panels. The screen is read from the panels only, never from the walk-through drawer, so the check cannot pass by finding the sentence that makes the claim. A figure that drifted would fail the harness rather than quietly misinform a reader.

Group 19 also asserts the content’s internal consistency: every step carries all of its required fields, and a step that tells the reader to click a named preset must carry that preset’s own parameters — so the instruction and the figures cannot disagree. It then drives the drawer as a user would: open it, step forward, press Apply these settings, and confirm the real sliders moved and the scenario chip changed, rather than only the model behind them.

And a figure is checked for its qualifier, not only for its value

Group 19 proves every quoted figure is one the model actually produces. It cannot prove a figure is quoted with the qualifier this package requires — that is a property of the prose, not of the panels. The independent review of the walk-through found three instances of exactly that gap, including $341,065 travelling with none of its mandatory qualifier on a page where every component of a tenfold error was available to the reader.

So group 20 reads the walk-through content as text, section by section, and enforces the package’s own rules structurally:

  • any section quoting a basis-point figure must also state that it is the modeled retirement-plan employer contribution impact excluding the separately funded health insurance credit and COLA effects;
  • $341,065 must appear with “not a cost per veteran”, must be identified as the most favourable of the seven published profiles, and must not be called a maximum — the cell grid reaches about $419,000;
  • the no-rate-change conclusion must be restricted to the central case and the single-driver excursions, because the fiscal model states that at 1.16 bp a plan’s rate does change to the nearest basis point;
  • four withdrawn labels and readings must not reappear — “frozen-service” for that baseline, “closed and shrinking”, the § 51.1-142.2 B 3 pinpoint, and the inserted words “State or Federal” in 10 U.S.C. § 12736.

The section is the unit of the test because a section is what the drawer shows at once and what prints as a block: a qualifier two sections away does not travel with a screenshot. This group found two live defects the moment it was written — an unrestricted no-rate-change claim in step 1, which the review had located only in step 9 and the closing, and a withdrawn phrase reintroduced in step 9.

A third harness, because a stylesheet cannot be eyeballed

node test/print-check.js runs 42 checks across all five surfaces — the dashboard, the walk-through, the methodology page and the two shipped single-file builds. It loads each page, emulates print media over the DevTools protocol, and measures the rendered box of the disclaimer block. A zero-area rectangle or a null offsetParent fails, however the computed style reads. @media print hides the masthead, and display: block !important on a descendant of a display: none ancestor restores nothing — so a disclaimer nested inside the masthead vanishes from every printed and PDF-exported copy, taking the version stamp, the provenance legend and the attribution line with it. The walk-through carried that defect; so, it turned out, did this methodology page, and the printed dashboard carried no version stamp at all. All three are fixed, and the harness now asserts that the model disclaimer, the § 30-19.1:7 disclaimer, the attribution line and the bill and package version are all visible under print media on every surface.

Two implementation details exist only to make that reconciliation exact. Python’s round() is banker’s rounding and JavaScript’s Math.round is not, so the engine implements banker’s rounding for the one place it is observable — the § 51.1-166 D 4 COLA delay. And survival and annuity factors are memoised, which is what makes a slider feel immediate without approximating anything.

4 · Population and exposure

The model is a cell model, not an average-member model. Every calculation happens inside a cell and is only then weighted up, which is what lets the tool keep Plan 1 and Plan 2 separate and keep the age/service composition of purchasers intact when a user types in a participant count.

A cell is a tuple (employer plan, benefit structure, age, creditable service, fiscal year) with an exposed headcount. The three employer plans are ST (VRS State Employees), TC (VRS Teachers) and PS (Participating Political Subdivisions, aggregate). The benefit structures are P1, P2 and HY (hybrid, excluded by default per the decision resolved October 19, 2026).

How the grid is built

  1. Start from the published active-member distribution by age band and service band (SRC-18), and the published headcount and covered payroll by employer plan (SRC-15). ●Published Fact
  2. Allocate each age × service band to a benefit structure from service length, because VRS does not publish the age × service grid by plan: hybrid below 11.5 years, Plan 2 from 11.5 to 17.5, Plan 1 at 17.5 and above (ASM-10), with tenure dispersion over a five-point kernel (ASM-23) and the derived split calibrated to the published plan split (ASM-11, SRC-16). The proxy fails for members with purchased, non-contiguous or part-time service, whose total service does not match tenure; this is the largest single source of uncertainty in the population estimate. ◑Derived Estimate
  3. Construct the political-subdivision grid from the state grid’s shape, scaled to the published political-subdivision active count (SRC-15) and adjusted by the ratio of published average service — 8.3 years against 10.1 for state employees — so the constructed mean matches the published mean (ASM-12). VRS publishes no political-subdivision grid, so there is nothing to check this against: direction of error unquantified. ◑Derived Estimate
  4. Roll each cell forward across FY2029–FY2038 — ten cohort years, the fiscal model’s horizon — deterministically (age + 1, service + 1 per year), removing members who reach an unreduced threshold at the published retirement rates (SRC-23) and applying a flat 3.00% per annum for all other exits, with 2.00% and 5.00% as the sensitivity (ASM-13). No new entrants are added to either structure, because both are closed: Plan 1 on July 1, 2010 and Plan 2 on January 1, 2014.

    Correction, package version 1.5. Earlier versions of this page said the beneficiary class is “closed and shrinking” and called that the single most useful fact the package had for the money committees. That claim is withdrawn. It is not established on the statutory text: the second sentence of § 51.1-169 A makes persons who participate in — or are merely eligible to participate in — the optional retirement plans under §§ 51.1-126.1, 51.1-126.3, 51.1-126.4 and 51.1-126.7 ineligible for the hybrid plan, and each of those sections leaves a continuing route for a new hire into the VRS defined benefit plan in every hire year. Version 1.3 of the bill closes that route by drafting (clause B 1(v)), but the practical effect depends on administrative practice VRS has not confirmed. The accurate statement is that the class is substantially closing, with named exceptions under review — and this model does not apply the B 1(v) screen at all, so its population is overstated to that extent.

  5. Keep a cell only if it is exposed to a purchase opportunity: within four years or four Rule-of-90 points of an unreduced threshold, and not already eligible, because § 51.1-142.4 B 5 bars a member who has already attained eligibility from purchasing at all.

Every count in this tool is a cumulative ten-year cohort total, not an annual rate. “220 participants” means 220 members across FY2029–FY2038, roughly 22 a year. Reading it as an annual figure overstates the bill by a factor of ten.

The salary curve

A cell’s pay is read from the published age-banded payroll curve (SRC-19) at the member’s age in the base year and rolled forward at salary growth g:

S(emp, a, f) = curve[emp](a − f) × (1 + g)^f

curve[ST], curve[TC] = published payroll per active member by age band   (SRC-18, SRC-19)
curve[PS]            = curve[ST] × 0.8394
                       0.8394 = published PS average compensation / ST average compensation (SRC-15)

where f is years from the base date (FY2025) to the cell’s fiscal year. Pay is a property of the cell, so the model never applies one average salary to everybody.

The four screens are applied per employer plan, cell by cell, inside the aggregation — a teacher cell is screened at the teacher veteran share, not at a system-wide average. Panel G has to show a single blended veteran share at its third step, and that blend is therefore weighted by the exposed near-threshold headcount of each employer plan, which is the population the screens are actually applied to. Weighting it by total active headcount instead described a different population from the one reaching the later steps, and with the three veteran sliders set unevenly the funnel could widen between its last two steps. test/standalone.js group 11 asserts the funnel never widens, at six settings of the shares. Weighted this way the third step is identically Σemp exposed × share, so every step is a true subset of the one above it.

Moving the funnel screens alone takes the combined aggregate impact from 0.07 to 0.44 basis points. That spread is the honest measure of how much the model does not know, and it is why Panel G shows provenance on every step rather than only a total.

5 · Statutory eligibility — the formulas the bill actually operates on

The bridge credit counts toward two pathways to an unreduced allowance and nothing else: § 51.1-153 B 2 (age 50 with 30 years of creditable service) and § 51.1-153 B 3 (the Rule of 90). The Explorer labels these B2 and B3 throughout, and maps Plan 1 members to B 2 and Plan 2 members to B 3.

“Plan 1” and “Plan 2” are not statutory terms. Title 51.1 speaks of a “person who becomes a member on or after July 1, 2010” and of the 60-months-as-of-January-1-2013 test. The bill operates on § 51.1-153 B 2 and B 3; the plan names are used here only because that is what a member and a VRS counsellor say.

5.1 Eligibility deficit — § 51.1-142.4 C 1

deficit_B2(a, n) = max(0, 30 − n)              years of creditable service
deficit_B3(a, n) = max(0, 90 − (a + n))        Rule-of-90 points

5.2 Purchase amount — § 51.1-142.4 C 2

Three caps bind simultaneously: the deficit, the remaining room under the existing four-year aggregate military maximum, and the qualifying service the member actually has.

k = min( deficit,  max(0, 4 − prior § 51.1-142.2 purchase),  qualifying available )

rule D4a (the bill as drafted) : buy k
rule D4b (rejected alternative): buy k only if k ≥ deficit, else buy nothing

Rule D4a — partial purchase capped at the deficit — is the rule resolved on October 19, 2026 and is the default. D4b is retained in Advanced Assumptions only so the rejected alternative can be priced. A member who has already attained eligibility has deficit = 0 and therefore buys nothing, which is § 51.1-142.4 B 5 operating arithmetically.

5.3 First age of unreduced eligibility, with credit k

B2:  A(a, n, k) = min( max( a + max(0, 30 − n − k),  50 ),  65 )
B3:  A(a, n, k) = min( a + max(0, (90 − (a + n + k)) / 2),  SSA NRA )

Three features of these two lines carry the whole design of the bill.

  • The credit never increases attained age (§ 51.1-142.4 C 4). In B 2, k reduces the service shortfall but the max(·, 50) stands: a member at 44 with 26 years who buys four years still waits until 50.
  • The divisor of 2 in B 3 is the asymmetry nobody expects. Age and service each accrue a year per year worked, so the Rule-of-90 sum grows two points a year. One Plan 2 point therefore buys half a year of acceleration. Four bridge years buy up to four years of acceleration under B 2 but only two under B 3.
  • Acceleration is the difference the bill creates: acceleration = max(0, A(a, n, 0) − A(a, n, k)). This is the quantity the bridge actually sells.

5.4 Normal retirement ages

Plan 1 normal retirement age        = 65                  (§ 51.1-153 A; SRC-13)
Plan 2 / hybrid normal retirement   = Social Security NRA  (§ 51.1-153 A; SRC-14)
SSA NRA, birth year ≥ 1960         = 67
SSA NRA, 1954–1959                 = 66 + 2 months per year after 1954

Where a cell has no birth year — which is every aggregate cell — SSA NRA is taken as 67.

5.5 Early-retirement reduction factors — SRC-12

These are the factors the bridge removes, so they are the price of the thing being sold. VRS publishes the construction rather than a numeric table:

months = (first unreduced age − a) × 12
reduction = 0.005 × min(months, 60) + 0.004 × max(0, months − 60)
  if a < 55 (Plan 1 only):  reduction += 0.006 × (55 − a) × 12
factor = max(0, 1 − reduction)

With the statutory gates on whether the member may retire early at all:

  • Plan 1: ineligible if service < 5, or age < 50, or (age < 55 and service < 10) — the 55/5 and 50/10 rules.
  • Plan 2: ineligible if age < 60 or service < 5 — the 60/5 rule. VRS does not publish the numeric Plan 2 / hybrid factor table, so ASM-09 applies the Plan 1 construction as a proxy. This is safe in practice: below 60 a Plan 2 member cannot retire at all, so for this population no factor is needed and none is used.

Where the factor is zero — the member cannot retire under current law at the accelerated date at all — the comparison baseline becomes a deferred unreduced allowance from normal retirement age (SRC-07), not a reduced allowance. That case is the one where the bridge is worth the most.

6 · The price — approximate normal cost

§ 51.1-142.4 D prices the purchase at approximate normal cost applied to creditable compensation or average final compensation at the time of purchase, whichever is higher:

price = ANC[plan] × k × max( S,  AFC_now )
Approximate normal cost rates, effective July 1, 2025 (SRC-08). These are the rates for ordinary non-hazardous members.

The applicable approximate-normal-cost rate for eligible pre-2014 § 51.1-153 B 4 public-safety members — local firefighters, police and EMS in resolution localities, who remain potentially eligible under the bill — requires VRS confirmation. The tool prices them at the ordinary non-hazardous rate, which is the wrong rate if VRS certifies a hazardous-duty normal cost. This is an open item, not a modelling choice.

Why the price is the wrong price, structurally

Approximate normal cost is the price of a year of accrual — it is calibrated to the cost of one more year in the benefit formula. The bridge sells something else entirely: removal of the actuarial early-retirement reduction, with no increase in benefit-calculation service. The model therefore prices the purchase at ANC because that is what the bill says, and separately measures what the member receives (§ 9). The gap between those two is the subsidy, and surfacing it is the main purpose of this tool.

7 · Benefit mechanics

7.1 Average final compensation — ASM-08

AFC is the average of the highest consecutive months of creditable compensation: 36 months for Plan 1, 60 for Plan 2 and hybrid (SRC-11). Rolling a current salary forward f years and averaging backwards over m months:

S_at = S × (1 + g)^f

AFC(S, f, m, g) = (1/m) × Σmo=0..m−1  S_at × (1 + g)^−(mo + 0.5)/12

The half-month offset places each month’s pay at its midpoint. With g = 0 the expression collapses to S_at, as it should.

7.2 The multiplier — SRC-09, SRC-10

Plan 1   1.70%
Hybrid   1.00%
Plan 2   service-weighted blend:
         pre  = min(service before January 1, 2013, n)
         post = max(0, n − pre)
         multiplier = (pre × 1.70% + post × 1.65%) / n

For aggregate Plan 2 cells, service before January 1, 2013 is taken as 2.5 years (ASM-24): Plan 2 membership dates run from January 1, 2008 to December 31, 2013, so pre-2013 service lies between 0 and 5 years, and the midpoint is used. Representative profiles carry their own figure.

7.3 The allowance — § 51.1-155 A 1, and the line that makes this an eligibility benefit

allowance = multiplier(plan, n) × n × AFC

Here n is actual creditable service only. The purchased eligibility credit k appears nowhere in this formula. That is not a simplification — it is the bill:

  • the § 51.1-124.3 amendment carves eligibility credit out of the definition of “creditable service,” which protects the benefit formula, AFC, the health insurance credit threshold and amount, group life service thresholds, disability allowances and the five-year vesting analogue in one stroke;
  • § 51.1-142.4 E and F 3 say so again expressly for the credit itself.

If a future draft let k into this one line, the bill would become an accrual benefit and every figure in this tool would understate it.

What the bridge does not increase: benefit-calculation service, the pension formula, or any service-credit-based benefit. What remains applicable: the ordinary consequences of an earlier retirement date. Those consequences are real, and §§ 13 and 19 state them rather than claiming the bridge has no effect on any other VRS benefit.

8 · Mortality, survival and annuity factors

8.1 Base mortality

Published quinquennial rates are used for each employer plan, sex, and status: healthy post-retirement (SRC-20) and pre-retirement (SRC-21), from the VRS valuation’s Pub-2010 based tables. Between published ages, rates are interpolated log-linearly (ASM-01), which preserves the geometric shape of a mortality curve where linear interpolation would not:

w    = (x − ai) / (ai+1 − ai)
q(x) = q(ai) × ( q(ai+1) / q(ai) )^w

Above the top published age — 90 — the last log-slope is extended and the rate is capped at 0.60, with a terminal age of 115 (ASM-02). Pub-2010 actually decelerates at the oldest ages, so this is mildly conservative:

slope = ln( q(atop) / q(atop−1) ) / (atop − atop−1)
q(x)  = min( 0.60,  q(atop) × e^slope × (x − atop) )

8.2 Mortality improvement — ASM-05

A flat annual improvement from the Pub-2010 base year, used as a tractable proxy for 75% of scale MP-2021:

q(x, Y) = min( 0.60,  q_base(x) × (1 − i)^max(0, Y − 2010) )      i = 0.75% central

Sex is blended 50/50 (ASM-03), because the cell grid carries no sex split.

8.3 Survival

p0 = 1 ;   pt+1 = pt × ( 1 − q(x + t, Y + t) )      to terminal age 115

Pre-retirement rates apply while the member is assumed still active, that is up to the assumed commencement age; healthy post-retirement rates apply thereafter.

8.4 The annuity factor

Present value at commencement of 1 per annum payable for life, COLA-indexed, monthly-in-advance approximated as an annuity-due with a half-year timing adjustment (ASM-04):

v = 1 / (1 + d)

ä  =  (1 + d)^−0.5  ×  Σt≥0  pt × (1 + c)^N(t) × v^t

N(t) = max( 0,  (t + 1) − (colaFirstYear − 1) − delay )

colaFirstYear = 3 by default (ASM-06): the statutory supplement is granted on July 1 of the second calendar year after retirement, so the model applies the first increase at the start of annuity year 3 and compounds annually. COLA rates are 2.50% for Plan 1 and 2.25% for Plan 2 and hybrid (SRC-05).

8.5 The COLA delay under § 51.1-166 D 4 — and why it is frozen

For a retiree with fewer than 20 years of creditable service, the post-retirement supplement begins a year after the date the member would first have been eligible for an unreduced allowance. Left alone, the bridge would have moved that date earlier and started the COLA earlier — one of three leaks the concept paper did not identify. The conforming amendment freezes the date, and the model implements the freeze rather than the leak:

delay = (service at retirement < 20)  ?  acceleration  :  0
      rounded with banker's rounding, to match the Python model exactly

In practice almost no purchaser has fewer than 20 years of creditable service, so this term is usually zero. That is itself the finding recorded in § 13: the § 51.1-166 D 4 amendment protects a class this population is essentially never in, which is why the COLA commencement exposure for everyone else remains open and unquantified.

8.6 Deferred annuity factor

For a benefit commencing n years in the future, with survival to commencement interpolated geometrically between integral ages:

n       = commencement age − current age
p_defer = p⌊n⌋ × ( p⌊n⌋+1 / p⌊n⌋ )^n − ⌊n⌋

factor  = p_defer × ä(commencement) / (1 + d)^n

9 · What the member receives — two baselines, and φ

“Value” is meaningless without stating what it is value against. The model computes one proposed outcome and two counterfactuals, and never averages them silently.

9.1 The proposed outcome

The member retires as soon as the purchased credit confers eligibility, at age a₁ = A(a, n, k):

f               = a₁ − a                                  years worked to that date
service         = n + f                                   actual creditable service
AFC             = AFC(S, f, m, g)
annual          = multiplier × service × AFC
PVprop         = annual × deferred annuity factor to a₁, with the COLA delay

9.2 Baseline SUB — “frozen service / leaving anyway”

The member leaves on the same date with the same service and the same AFC, but under current law takes the actuarially reduced allowance:

PVsub = annual × reductionFactor(a₁, service) × (same annuity factor)

valueSUB = PVprop − PVsub

This isolates exactly what the bridge sells: removal of the reduction, nothing else. Where the member could not retire at all at that date, the baseline becomes a deferred unreduced allowance from normal retirement age instead (SRC-07), which is a larger value.

9.3 Baseline WTE — “work to eligibility”

The member works on under current law to first unreduced eligibility at a₀ = A(a, n, 0) and retires then — earning more service, a higher AFC, and a larger allowance:

f₀            = a₀ − a
PVwte        = multiplier × (n + f₀) × AFC(S, f₀, m, g) × annuity factor at a₀

valueWTE = PVprop − PVwte

Against this baseline the member gives up accrual, AFC growth and years of contributions, so the value of the bridge is far smaller — and frequently negative net of the price.

9.4 The blend, and what φ means

value = φ × valueWTE  +  (1 − φ) × valueSUB

net liability = value − price
φ (ASM-22) is the share of purchasers who would otherwise have worked on to unreduced eligibility. Central 50%; the slider spans 0–100%.
WhoWeightConsequence
Frozen-service / leaving anyway1 − φ May receive a substantial subsidy from accelerated unreduced retirement — the reduction is removed from an allowance they would have taken anyway.
Work-to-eligibilityφ May pay more than the additional actuarial value received, because they forgo accrual and AFC growth they would have earned.

φ is an assumption, not a finding, and it decides the sign of the per-member result. No published data bears on it. Worse, it is not even exogenous: a member decides whether to buy after knowing which type they are, and self-selection runs toward the subsidised end. Self-selection is not modeled. A reader who wants one number should take the range across φ, and should treat the central case as the optimistic end of it.

9.5 Contributions and payroll forgone

Over the acceleration window the employer loses the member’s payroll and the contributions on it. Present-valued at the scenario discount rate:

PV payroll forgone = Σt=0..⌊w⌋−1  S × (1+g)^f+t / (1+d)^f+t
                   + (w − ⌊w⌋) × S × (1+g)^f+⌊w⌋ / (1+d)^f+⌊w⌋
                                                     where w = acceleration years

total normal cost forgone     = PV payroll forgone × total normal cost rate
member contributions forgone  = PV payroll forgone × member contribution rate
employer DB contrib. forgone  = PV payroll forgone × employer DB rate

These feed Panel C. They are payroll quantities and never enter the contribution-rate arithmetic in § 11.

9.6 The $341,065 figure, used correctly

$341,065 is the modeled net value above the member purchase price for illustrative profile A — a Plan 1 state employee four years short of thirty at the minimum § 51.1-153 B 2 age. It is the most favourable of the seven published profiles, not a universal cost per veteran or a model-wide maximum, and must not be multiplied by a participant count. The underlying 1,025-cell grid reaches approximately $419,000 in its most favourable single cell. The model’s companion finding is equally part of the result: members who otherwise would have worked to unreduced eligibility overpay in the modeled WTE scenarios. Panel E reports both signs for both example members, at the selected φ, for exactly this reason.

10 · Aggregation and scaling

10.1 Probability-weighted cell result

How much qualifying service a member has is unknown, so each cell is evaluated over a distribution (ASM-25) rather than at a point:

1 year  10%        2 years 15%        3 years 20%        4 years 55%

Each branch is evaluated separately and weighted. Branches where the member would buy nothing are dropped, and the surviving weight becomes pBuy, the probability that a member of this cell purchases at all. Selecting Fixed average in the dashboard replaces the distribution with a single stated amount, separately for Plan 1 years and Plan 2 points.

10.2 From exposure to participants

pool                = exposed headcount × purchasableShare(employer)
purchasable         = Σcells  pool × pBuy
participants        = purchasable × take-up

Every monetary quantity is accumulated on the purchasable pool and scaled once at the end, which is what keeps Plan 1 and Plan 2 separate until the last step.

10.3 Direct participant entry preserves the mix

When a legislator types “400 Plan 1 participants,” the tool does not build an average member and multiply by 400. It rescales the existing age × service × salary mix:

scale[plan] = requested count / purchasable[plan]

The composition of purchasers — and so the mean deficit, mean acceleration and price per participant — is unchanged. Group 6 of the regression harness checks exactly this.

10.4 A salary override is an exact multiplier, not an approximation

Every monetary output of a case is homogeneous of degree one in salary: the purchase amount, every age, and every reduction factor are independent of pay. So an average-salary override can be applied post hoc, exactly:

multiplier[plan] = requested mean salary / modeled mean salary

price, valueSUB, valueWTE, payroll forgone, contributions  ×= multiplier
participants, years purchased, acceleration                   unchanged

This is why the dashboard responds instantly to a salary change, and why a Plan 1 override provably leaves every Plan 2 figure untouched (harness group 7). It is an algebraic identity, not a shortcut.

11 · From liability to an employer contribution rate

A liability is amortised into a rate by VRS’s layered level-percent-of-payroll method. ASM-20 mirrors that method; it is not VRS’s calculation.

net   = (1 + d) / (1 + j) − 1                 j = payroll growth (3.00%, SRC-04)
v     = 1 / (1 + net)
F(N)  = (1 − v^N) / net × (1 + net)            N = published amortisation period
                                              (SRC-15; 20 years for state, teacher
                                               and the political subdivision aggregate)

basis points = 10,000 × ( ΔL / F(N) ) / covered payroll

11.1 Two different basis-point figures, and which one is which

A contribution rate is certified per employer plan, not per benefit structure. Both views are shown because they answer different questions.
ViewPayroll baseReading
By benefit structure (Plan 1 / Plan 2) Combined covered payroll of all three employer plans, $26.05bn, 20 years That structure’s additive contribution to the combined aggregate. These figures sum to the combined figure.
By employer plan (ST / TC / PS) That plan’s own payroll, own amortisation period, own payroll growth The rate a payroll office would actually see, added to the published employer DB rate.

11.2 Why the discount rate and the amortisation rate are separate controls

By default the liability layer is amortised at the Board’s published 6.75% even when the user moves the discount rate. That is what the committed model does in its own discount-rate sensitivity, and it is the right treatment: moving the discount rate is a sensitivity on how the liability is measured, not a change in the Board’s funding policy. A user who wants both to move can tick the box in Advanced Assumptions — at which point the tool stops reproducing the published sensitivity table, which is why it is off by default and labelled.

11.3 The 0.19 basis-point figure

The central-case figure of 0.19 basis points is the modeled retirement-plan employer contribution impact. It excludes separately funded health insurance credit and COLA effects, including unquantified COLA exposure. It is complete for bill version 1.1; a refresh against the bill as introduced is required before external use.

That qualifier travels with the figure everywhere it appears in this package — in the dashboard, in the downloadable handout, and here. A basis-point number quoted without it is being quoted wrongly.

12 · Payroll and workforce effects — and the guardrails

Panel C quantities arise from a hiring decision, not from the pension change. They are labelled employer workforce/payroll effects, not pension savings, and they are kept out of every contribution-rate figure.

departing        = PV payroll forgone                   (§ 9.5)
not replaced     = departing × ν
refilled base    = departing × (1 − ν)
replacement      = refilled base × ρ
lower-paid diff  = refilled base − replacement  =  refilled base × (1 − ρ)
mean window      = accelerated years / participants      (§ 9.5)
annual refilled  = refilled base / mean window
vacancy          = min( annual refilled × ρ × months/12,  replacement )

employer DB on departing    = departing × employer DB rate
employer DB on replacement  = (replacement − vacancy) × employer DB rate
employer DB differential    = the difference of those two

payroll / workforce effect  = lower-paid diff + not replaced + vacancy
                            + employer DB differential

One reading note, because the label is shorter than the quantity: the “Replacement payroll” tile in Panel C reports replacement − vacancy, not replacement — what the employer actually pays the replacement, net of the months the post sat empty. With the vacancy slider at zero the two are identical, which is the default.

12.1 The guardrails, stated as the tests that enforce them

  1. A refilled position never produces a full-salary saving. Only the differential counts. At ρ = 90%, 10% of compensation counts, not 100%.
  2. At ρ = 100% the lower-paid differential is exactly zero. The total payroll effect is exactly zero only when ν = 0 and vacancy is zero. Otherwise it consists of the separately identified non-replacement, vacancy and related employer-contribution effects. Harness group 9 asserts the full grid.
  3. No Panel C control moves any pension figure. The harness checks the net pension liability is bit-identical at ρ = 100%, 75%, and at ν = 10%. A legislator can verify this live by dragging the replacement slider and watching Panel B not move.
  4. A non-replaced position is reported separately from lower-paid replacements, because it is a workforce-reduction decision with different evidence behind it — and it is still not pension savings.
  5. A vacancy is a one-off timing effect, valued on the replacement’s own compensation and capped at it, and reported separately from permanent reductions. The annualization step matters: departing is a present value over the whole acceleration window, which averages about 1.9 years, so months/12 has to be applied to an annual rate. Applying it to the window total instead valued a 12-month vacancy as the entire window — roughly twice the compensation actually avoided — and drove replacement payroll to zero as though the post were never filled. test/standalone.js group 10 asserts that a vacancy of n months never exceeds n months of modeled pay, and that a refilled post still draws pay whenever the vacancy is shorter than the window.
  6. Nothing is included that is not supported by data. Recruiting cost, onboarding cost, productivity ramp-up, institutional-knowledge loss and service-delivery effects are not modeled. Their absence does not mean they are zero; it means the model has no basis for a number, and a figure invented for them would be the easiest thing in this package to attack.

12.2 The replacement ladder

Panel C reports the payroll effect at 75%, 80%, 85%, 90%, 95% and 100% replacement simultaneously, with the selected setting marked. Showing the whole ladder rather than one point is deliberate: it makes the linearity of the assumption visible. At 100% replacement, the lower-paid differential is zero; the total is zero only with nothing eliminated and no vacancy. Each rung recomputes its own vacancy and related employer-contribution effect.

13 · Health insurance credit and COLA — outside every rate figure

HIC and COLA follow the retirement date, so preventing earlier commencement would require separate statutory changes outside the bridge. The proposal deliberately accepts and discloses the earlier commencement rather than adding separate delay provisions — the decision resolved October 20, 2026.

Both run on separately funded trusts and therefore sit outside every basis-point figure in this tool. The bill’s exclusion architecture reaches the threshold and amount of the health insurance credit — via the § 51.1-124.3 carve-out and § 51.1-142.4 F 8 — but neither verb reaches duration, and commencement follows the retirement date rather than creditable service.

13.1 The health insurance credit estimate

The estimate applies each employer plan’s statutory credit: $4.25 per month per year for state employees (§ 51.1-1400), $4.00 for teachers (§ 51.1-1401), and $1.50 capped at $45 per month for political subdivisions (§ 51.1-1402). Political-subdivision participation is employer-elective, so that cohort is an upper bound. An earlier retirement starts the applicable credit earlier:

annual    = min(service at retirement × employer-plan monthly rate, applicable cap) × 12
PV factor = Σt=0..⌊w⌋−1 v^t  +  (w − ⌊w⌋) × v^⌊w⌋        w = mean acceleration
HIC PV    = annual × PV factor × participants

Illustratively about $5,300 for a state retiree with 26 years over a four-year acceleration. This is a simple commencement-window present value at the scenario discount rate, not a trust valuation.

13.2 The COLA exposure is not quantified, and the tool says so

No figure in this model, and no figure in the fiscal model it is built on, quantifies the cost of commencing the post-retirement supplement up to four years earlier. The engine carries an explicit colaQuantified = false flag, and harness group 9 asserts the tool never reports a COLA number. This is the single largest disclosed gap in the package. Enactment clause 3(vi) requires the Board to report both costs separately by trust, which is the proper route to a number.

14 · Panel D — three totals, deliberately not merged

1. Modeled pension impact        = net liability change  (§ 9.4),  and its bp (§ 11)
2. Modeled payroll/workforce     = the Panel C total     (§ 12)
3. Combined scenario effect      = (2) − (1)

The combined figure is shown last and smallest on purpose. It is the least reliable of the three, because it adds a pension measurement to a workforce assumption — two quantities with different units of credibility. The panel carries the required warning verbatim:

Combined fiscal effect is a scenario result, not an actuarial certification or VRS fiscal impact statement.

No output anywhere in the tool is labelled “Savings.” The permitted labels are Modeled pension impact, Modeled payroll effect, Modeled workforce effect and Combined scenario effect.

18 · Palette, charts and accessibility

Colour carries three distinct kinds of meaning and the roles are never mixed:

  • Categorical — identity. Three slots only: Plan 1, Plan 2, combined/hybrid. Plan 1 is the same blue in every panel, every chart and every table.
  • Diverging — polarity. The tornado arms only, where the two ends mean opposite directions.
  • Status — provenance. Published Fact, Derived Estimate, Scenario Assumption. Always shipped with an icon and a text label, never colour alone, so the distinction survives greyscale printing and colour vision deficiency.

18.1 The palette is measured, not eyeballed

Both the three categorical slots and the two diverging arms were checked computationally in both light and dark mode, against their own surface, on five measures: OKLCH lightness band, OKLCH chroma floor, OKLab ΔE separation under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia, the normal-vision separation floor, and WCAG contrast against the chart surface. All five pass in both modes.

One result changed the design rather than being written up as acceptable: the combined/hybrid green measured 2.74:1 against the light surface, below the 3:1 floor. The dashboard does supply the required relief — every chart has a legend and a text table carrying the same values — but relief is a mitigation, not a fix, so the colour was darkened to #18a06f, which clears 3:1 while keeping ΔE 8.6 against the Plan 2 orange under protanopia and 27.2 under normal vision. Worst-case colour-vision separation across the categorical slots is ΔE 8.6–9.4, inside the floor band, which is legal only with the secondary encoding the dashboard already carries.

Light and dark are each defined explicitly rather than derived by inversion. Numbers use tabular figures so columns align. Every chart has a text table beside it carrying the same values, so nothing is available only as a picture — which also means the whole dashboard prints usefully. The print stylesheet drops the controls and the tab strip and keeps the panels, and both pages are laid out single-column below 1080 px for use on a tablet in a committee room.

19 · Known limitations

Stated plainly, because a reviewer who finds these unaided discounts everything else.

  1. φ is unknowable and self-selection is not modeled. It decides the sign of the per-member result, and selection runs toward the subsidised end. The central case is the optimistic end of the range (§ 9.4).
  2. The veteran funnel rests on one published number. Only the 6% state share is published; the teacher and political-subdivision shares are assumed. The funnel alone spans 0.07–0.44 basis points.
  3. The COLA commencement exposure is unquantified by any figure in this package (§ 13.2).
  4. The approximate normal cost rate for eligible pre-2014 B 4 public-safety members is unconfirmed. They are priced at the ordinary non-hazardous rate (§ 6).
  5. The fiscal model is complete for bill version 1.1; a refresh against the bill as introduced is required before external use (§ 1), and the version 1.3 clause B 1(v) scope screen is not applied here at all.
  6. The Plan 2 reduction factor table is a proxy (ASM-09). It does not bind for this population, but it is still a proxy.
  7. Mortality improvement is a flat-rate proxy for a two-dimensional scale (ASM-05), and sex is blended 50/50 (ASM-03) because the grid carries no sex split.
  8. Amortisation mirrors VRS’s method but is not VRS’s calculation (ASM-20), and the horizon is FY2029–FY2038 only — a ten-year cohort window, not a projection to the end of the liability.
  9. Second-order workforce costs are excluded, not valued at zero (§ 12.1 item 6).
  10. The sensitivity panel is one-at-a-time, and the drivers are not independent. Compounding them goes outside every bar on the chart: the fiscal model’s published compound-adverse case reaches 1.16 basis points (§ 15). A reader who adds the individual swings, or who reads the widest single bar as the widest case, understates the range.
  11. Federal tax treatment is unresolved. IRC § 415(n)(3)(A) may not reach this credit because it buys no accrual, which raises § 415(c) exposure and removes 403(b)/457(b) trustee-to-trustee funding — which decides who can afford the purchase at all. That belongs in front of veterans’ legal counsel with the text.

    Correction, package version 1.5 — 10 U.S.C. § 12736 is no longer an open item. Earlier versions of this page said § 12736 “bars excluding non-regular-retirement service in determining his eligibility for a state benefit, which is this bill’s entire subject.” That quotation was wrong and the conclusion drawn from it is withdrawn. The section contains no reference to State law, and the earlier quotation omitted its closing proviso — “if that service is otherwise properly credited under it.” § 12736 is an anti-exclusion rule, not a crediting mandate: it stops a plan carving out chapter 1223 service the plan already properly credits. Virginia credits prior active duty service for § 51.1-153 B 2 and B 3 purposes only on purchase, so there is nothing for § 12736 to operate on. It does not preempt this bill.

  12. One narrow policy question is open, and it is narrower than it was: whether a member on an approved leave of absence without pay counts as remaining in service under subdivision E 3. Version 1.3 answers yes — a member is not deemed to have terminated service by reason of an approved leave or of benefits under § 51.1-1112 or § 51.1-1123 — deliberately, because version 1.3’s continuity-of-service requirement would otherwise have created a new trap for a member who took unpaid leave years before retiring. That is the member-protective reading, it is a provisional position rather than a settled one, and reversing it is a one-clause change. The long-term-disability half of this question is withdrawn: §§ 51.1-1110 H, 51.1-1117 C and 51.1-1117 A appear to answer it already, so it is now a one-line VRS confirmation item. Nothing in this model turns on either answer.

All principal policy questions are settled. The seven decisions of October 19–20, 2026 are drafted into the bill. What remains is confirmation work — VRS, the actuary, DLS and veterans’ legal counsel — plus the narrow approved-leave / long-term-disability question above.
